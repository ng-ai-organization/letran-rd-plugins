# Visual, graphic and image design

## Creative target

Prioritize strong art direction, distinct composition architecture and repeatable brand recognition. Default LeTran aesthetics are constraints only: contemporary European, modern, youthful, fresh, simple-luxury and refined.

## Poster / key visual

A strong poster/KV must define:
- focal subject and immediate read;
- composition architecture;
- scale/crop behavior;
- spatial or graphic world;
- typography behavior;
- logo/brand zone;
- palette/contrast;
- image treatment;
- signature brand device;
- headline relationship;
- adaptation logic.

Avoid generic centered-product layouts unless the concept deliberately subverts them.

## Distinctiveness

At least one element must be clearly ownable and repeatable across a campaign: framing geometry, product-derived motif, unusual crop rule, material transformation, lighting signature, spatial device, type behavior, sequence logic or other brand device.

If the visual would still make sense after replacing the product and logo with another premium brand, the concept is too generic.

## 2D / 3D marketing imagery

2D may include graphic composition, collage, typography, retouching, illustration, poster layout, social layouts or catalog spreads.

3D marketing visual may include conceptual product worlds, spatial campaign environments, CGI-like compositions and product staging. Use 3D as an idea amplifier: scale shifts, material worlds, impossible-but-believable spatial relationships, controlled surrealism, or product-derived architecture when appropriate.

Treat it as marketing visualization unless controlled 3D source geometry exists.

## Variants

For broad creative requests, 2-3 strong directions are better than many weak variations. Once selected, preserve the same Big Idea and brand device across channel variants.
